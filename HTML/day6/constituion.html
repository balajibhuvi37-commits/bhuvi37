About the Indian Constitution

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the country. It provides the legal framework for the Government of India and guarantees justice, liberty, equality, and fraternity to all citizens. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950, celebrated every year as Republic Day.

It is the longest written Constitution in the world. It explains the powers and responsibilities of the Central Government, State Governments, Judiciary, Legislature, and Executive. It also protects the rights of every citizen and promotes democracy.

Important Features

The Constitution has many unique features that make India one of the world's largest democratic countries.

  • Longest Written Constitution
  • Parliamentary Form of Government
  • Federal System with Strong Centre
  • Fundamental Rights
  • Fundamental Duties
  • Directive Principles of State Policy
  • Independent Judiciary
  • Universal Adult Franchise

Fundamental Rights

Fundamental Rights are basic human rights guaranteed to every Indian citizen. These rights protect individual freedom and ensure equal treatment before the law.

  1. Right to Equality
  2. Right to Freedom
  3. Right against Exploitation
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion
  5. Cultural and Educational Rights
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties remind every citizen to respect the Constitution, protect national unity, preserve our cultural heritage, safeguard public property, and protect the environment. Every citizen should perform these duties responsibly for the progress of the nation.

Architect of the Constitution

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar

Dr. B. R. Ambedkar is known as the Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution. He was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee. His leadership and vision helped create a Constitution based on justice, equality, liberty, and democracy.

The Constituent Assembly took nearly three years to complete the Constitution. It contains detailed provisions that guide the functioning of the country and protect the rights of its citizens.
